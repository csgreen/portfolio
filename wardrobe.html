Independent AI Product Exploration · July–November 2026

From Conversation to System:
Building an AI Decision Assistant

Product strategy · Applied AI · Decision intelligence · Personalization · Data integration

The opportunity. Many everyday decisions take more time and mental effort than they should. The information needed to make a good decision often exists, but it is scattered across sources, difficult to recall, or hard to evaluate together.

I explored how AI could bring together multiple data sources, apply relevant knowledge and rules, visualize options, and help people make better decisions faster.

Product hypothesis

A context-aware AI assistant could reduce decision time while improving the quality and consistency of the outcome.

01 / Test case

A real decision problem

I chose personal presentation as the test case. How I present myself is part of my personal and professional brand, but consistent choices require balancing available options, context, established guidelines, and individual preferences.

Even as a visual thinker, I found it difficult to picture how separate elements would work together or remember which combinations and conventions worked best. The goal was not a styling tool for its own sake, but a reusable approach to reducing decision friction.

02 / My approach

Prototype in real use

I used ChatGPT as a rapid prototyping environment, developing the experience through real-world use instead of building standalone software upfront. Each iteration addressed a specific weakness in the decision process.

01

Model the decision

Identify available assets, preferences, guidelines, fit, context, and practical constraints; translate them into reusable rules.

02

Integrate information

Bring together records, images, purchase history, expert color analysis, weather, and activities.

03

Make expertise actionable

Explain guidelines, suggest complete solutions, and use visuals to evaluate combinations.

04

Improve reliability

Address stale memory and inaccurate references with an editable Google Sheets master inventory.

05

Iterate from failures

Refine accuracy, relevance, variety, and visual fidelity based on observed use and corrections.

03 / Key product decisions

Optimize for learning and trust

Start with an existing AI platform

Validate the problem and interaction model before investing in custom software.

Combine multiple data types

Support decisions that depend on records, imagery, expert guidance, and changing context.

Externalize knowledge and decision rules

Reduce cognitive load and make specialized guidance consistently accessible.

Establish a structured source of truth

Reduce errors from stale, incomplete, or inconsistent conversational memory.

Prioritize trust over autonomy

Improve recommendation quality before automating consequential actions.

04 / What I learned

Useful AI is more than answers

The most important insight: AI creates value when it turns fragmented information and specialized knowledge into decisions people can act on with confidence.

Data reliability

Recommendations are only as useful as the information they are grounded in.

Context & state

Systems must distinguish lasting preferences, temporary conditions, and changes over time.

Human-centered interaction

Explanations and visualizations build confidence and preserve user control.

05 / Outcome

A working personalized decision-support workflow

I combined structured inventory data, visual references, contextual inputs, and reusable decision guidelines to translate scattered information into practical recommendations—reducing the need to repeatedly recall rules or manually evaluate combinations.

Next: measure decision time and recommendation quality, improve data synchronization, and introduce carefully scoped automation.

Longer-term vision

From assistant to agentic system

Build toward an agentic experience that could retrieve context, reconcile information, evaluate options, and propose actions with appropriate human oversight. The current project is a working AI-assisted workflow, not a deployed autonomous agent.

Broader application

Enterprise decision intelligence

Employees face similar challenges: fragmented data, specialized knowledge, complex rules, and limited time. The opportunity is to reduce cognitive load, make expertise accessible at the point of decision, and improve efficiency and outcomes while preserving accuracy, transparency, and control.
