Building a Context-Aware AI Recommendation System

I designed and iterated on a system that brings together structured data, images, conversational context, and changing user preferences to recommend practical next actions.

The product challenge: An AI assistant can produce a plausible answer. Can it produce the right answer when the underlying data changes, sources disagree, and the user keeps refining what they need?

01 / Why I built it

Personalized AI breaks down when context is fragmented.

I wanted to test a common problem in AI products: users have information spread across different formats and systems, while their needs and constraints change constantly. A conversational model can answer individual questions, but useful ongoing assistance requires dependable state, relevant retrieval, and a way to correct mistakes.

I chose a personal wardrobe as the test domain because it offers a surprisingly demanding set of inputs: purchase records, product imagery, changing ownership, color preferences, fit, availability, weather, and qualitative feedback. The domain was a test bed for the system design—not the product's defining idea.

03 / Product decisions

The hard part was reliability, not generation.

Separate historical records from current truth.

A purchase is not proof that an item is still owned. I maintained explicit inventory and exclusion decisions so recommendations could reflect updated state.

Preserve visual provenance.

Generic lookalike imagery created misleading confidence. I prioritized references to actual user-supplied items and clear disclosure when visual fidelity was unavailable.

Make uncertainty visible.

Color classification and ambiguous records need verification states instead of forced answers. The underlying inventory distinguishes close matches, poor matches, and uncertain cases.

Turn corrections into requirements.

Repeated suggestions, unavailable items, and missing practical details exposed gaps in the workflow. Those failures informed more specific constraints and quality checks.

05 / What I learned

Trustworthy AI needs product infrastructure.

Data reliability is part of the experience. Even polished recommendations fail when stale or ambiguous facts are treated as authoritative.

Persistent context is a product design problem. Preferences, records, and corrections need clear ownership and update paths—not simply longer prompts.

Evaluation must reflect actual usefulness. A next step is measuring whether recommendations are valid, actionable, diverse, and grounded in the right sources.

Agentic behavior is a roadmap, not a label. The current system is an AI-assisted, human-operated workflow. A future application could automate ingestion, retrieval, state reconciliation, and evaluation with appropriate user controls.

The bigger opportunity

One test domain. A broadly applicable product problem.

This pattern applies wherever recommendations depend on multiple evolving sources: customer success, operations, professional development, procurement, and personal decision support. The transferable insight is how to design for state, relevance, and trust.
